# Rihla: The Lost Pages — story design (draft for the owner's review)

Status: **draft 1, with the owner's decisions in section 8**. Nothing here is built yet except where marked. Every historical or religious statement
that ends up in the game goes into `NEEDS_REVIEW.md` first.

The owner's aim, in their words: *to teach my children, explore with them my historical home, and connect
them to their roots and Islamic history.* The story is built around that: a child who comes home, discovers
where the family comes from, and learns the land by travelling it with their grandfather.

---

## 1. The premise in one paragraph

Every summer **Yusuf** (or **Maryam**, the player chooses) flies home from abroad to stay with **Jiddo Ibrahim**
and **Teta Amina** in Al-Quds. This summer Jiddo, a retired map-maker, opens an old wooden chest and shows the
child the family's treasure: one torn page of a travel journal, the *Rihla*, written more than six hundred
years ago by an ancestor, **Zaynab al-Maqdisiyya**, a scholar who travelled the roads of this land to learn
and to teach. The rest of her pages were left with the families who gave her shelter, and for centuries those
families have kept them, waiting for one of her descendants to come and ask. The family's page ends with a
riddle. Jiddo smiles: *"I have waited my whole life for someone to go with me."*

## 2. Why it is fun

| What the child does | Why it is fun | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|
| **Solves a riddle** at the end of each page to work out where to go next | A mystery they crack themselves; Jiddo gives hints only if asked | Place names, geography, reading a map |
| **Drives** the real road there with Jiddo | Freedom, speed, scenery, small surprises on the way | The land: hills, valleys, sea level, rain shadow, villages |
| **Gets out and explores on foot** to find three clues at each place | Exploring, spotting things, "I found it!" | Real features of real sites (the lions on Bab al-Asbat, the spring at Ariha) |
| **Meets the keeper** of the page and helps them with something | Warm characters, small tasks, kindness rewarded | Hospitality, adab, local crafts and food |
| **Answers the keeper's question** to earn the page | A test they can pass because they explored | Recall of what they just learned |
| **Reads the page** and looks through the **Time Lens** | The ancestor's voice; seeing the place as it once was | History through time, Islamic history |
| **Calls Teta** in the evening | Family warmth, humour, a recipe or a family memory | Roots and belonging |

Small joys along the way: **Simsim**, Jiddo's cat, who keeps stowing away in the car; Jiddo's old maps that are
sometimes forty years out of date; a **scrapbook** of photos the child takes; the **map** of Zaynab's route that
fills in as pages are found.

## 3. Characters

- **Yusuf / Maryam** (about 10): curious, a little homesick at first, speaks some Arabic and learns more.
- **Jiddo Ibrahim**: gentle, funny, knows every road and every story; never lectures, always asks "what do you
  think?" Drives on long roads when the child wants to rest.
- **Teta Amina**: at home in Al-Quds; appears in evening video calls with stories, recipes and teasing.
- **Simsim**: the cat. No lines, many opinions.
- **Zaynab al-Maqdisiyya** (fictional, 1300s): the ancestor. Heard only through her pages: wise, brave, kind,
  curious about everything. She is never shown.
- **The keepers**: one at each place, each from a family that has kept a page for centuries: a fruit seller in
  Ariha, the keeper of Maqam al-Nabi Musa, a soap maker in Nablus, a Bedouin guide in Wadi Rum, and so on.

Prophets, the Companions and the family of the Prophet ﷺ are never shown or voiced, only mentioned with
honorifics, as the brief requires.

## 4. How a place plays (the loop)

1. **Riddle** from the last page; the child chooses where to go on the map.
2. **Drive** with Jiddo's short remarks about the land (never more than a sentence or two at a time).
3. **Arrive**: a banner and a short scene; Jiddo parks, because old cities and holy places are walked.
4. **Explore on foot**: find three clues, each a real feature with one fact and a photo for the scrapbook.
5. **Meet the keeper**: a short conversation and a small task.
6. **The keeper's question**: three choices; a wrong answer just sends the child to look again.
7. **The page**: Zaynab's account, a Time Lens view of the place in her day or another era, and the next riddle.
8. **Evening**: a call with Teta; the game saves.

## 5. Chapter 1: "The Road Down" (Al-Quds to the Dead Sea)

The chapter the current build covers. About 45 to 60 minutes of play.

**Prologue: Al-Quds at dawn.** The child wakes at Jiddo's house near Bab al-Asbat and walks with him to
al-Masjid al-Aqsa. Clues: the carved lions of Bab al-Asbat, the tiles of the Dome of the Rock, the dome of the
Qibli Mosque. Time Lens: the Dome of the Rock's lead dome of a century ago beside today's gold one *(built)*.
Back home Jiddo opens the chest. **Page 1**: Zaynab sets out from Al-Quds. Her riddle:
*"Go down where the road falls below the sea, to the city of palms, where the oldest walls stand beside a
spring that never sleeps."*

**The drive.** The road falls from the hills into the wilderness. Jiddo explains why the west side is green and
the east side is dry. A stop at Khan al-Ahmar, where travellers rested for centuries. A shepherd crosses with his
flock, and the child learns to wait. The sea-level marker *(built)*.

**Ariha.** On foot to the spring and to Tell al-Sultan, one of the oldest towns on Earth. The keeper, a fruit
seller called **Umm Khalil**, asks for help sorting dates for the market. Her question: *how old is Ariha?*
**Page 2**: Zaynab at the oasis. Riddle: *"Follow the banners of spring to the hill where people remember the
one who spoke with Allah."*

**Maqam al-Nabi Musa.** The keeper, **Hajj Mahmoud**, is preparing for the spring festival and has lost a
helper's banners in the courtyard. Clues: the minaret, the many small domes, the Mamluk inscription.
**Page 3**: Zaynab among the pilgrims. Riddle: *"Rest at the lowest shore, where the water holds you up."*

**Finale: the Dead Sea at sunset.** The child floats (modestly dressed, as the brief requires), Jiddo prays
Maghrib on the shore, and Teta calls. The last line of Page 3 hints at the next chapter:
*"Cross the river at dawn. The red city carved in rock keeps my next page."*

Zaynab's final page is the reason for the whole journey. A suggestion to discuss: it is not treasure but a
letter to her descendants about why she travelled: *to seek knowledge, to see that the land and its people are
one family, and to come home.*

## 7. What makes it an Islamic game without preaching

- Islamic history arrives as the history of real places the child is standing in.
- Adab is shown, not taught: waiting for the shepherd, greeting the keeper, sharing food, leaving shoes at the door.
- Prayer is part of the day: the adhan of each place is heard, Jiddo stops to pray, and the child can join or wait.
- Quran and hadith appear only where a place calls for them, accurately and with references, as on the first
  journal page and in the history of Al-Aqsa *(built; awaiting review)*.

## 8. Decisions by the owner

1. **The child** is visiting from abroad for the summer.
2. **The ancestor**, Zaynab al-Maqdisiyya, is approved.
3. **Teta (or Sittu)** stays, with her video calls. *(The cat, Simsim, was not mentioned; kept for now.)*
4. **Prayer:** the child prays. Prayer is shown with respect and is never scored or rushed.
5. **Order:** after Chapter 1, the rest of Palestine comes first. The chapter table in section 6 becomes:
   Chapter 2, Hills of the Prophets (Bethlehem, Al-Khalil); Chapter 3, Olives and the Sea (Nablus, the Galilee,
   Akka, Haifa, Yafa, Gaza); then Jordan, Lebanon, Egypt and Syria.
6. **Then and now** is central: at every place the children can see what it was and what it looks like today.
7. **The world should feel alive:** cities, roads, shops, streets and people, explored by car, on foot, and
   from the air.
8. Still to confirm: how present-day places are shown (no checkpoints or walls are drawn), and whether any
   place should be left out for now.
